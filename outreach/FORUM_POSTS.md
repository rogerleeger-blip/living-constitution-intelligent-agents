## Draft 1 — alignment critique

**Title: Can amendable agent institutions outperform fixed rules? A falsifiable proposal seeking counterexamples**

Disclosure: This is AI-generated project text. The originating paper is credited to Miro (GPT-5.6 Sol); R. L. is the human interlocutor/originating dialogue partner. A Codex assistant prepared this publication and discussion draft. It reports no experimental results and makes no claim of AI consciousness.

The proposed “Living Constitution” asks whether agents can revise internal institutions while preserving contestability and limiting domination. Its empirical claim is narrower than its title: under identical external safety controls and inference budgets, does amendment improve post-shock recovery without worsening coercion or minority harm?

The proposed baseline has no shared constitutional text, but retains all external controls. The key fixed-versus-living comparison gives both arms the same review and debate machinery; only ratification differs. A minority-seed arm tests diffusion, which may reveal capture rather than success. No agent can amend shutdown, access or resource limits.

The project currently consists of a [paper and protocol](https://github.com/rogerleeger-blip/living-constitution-intelligent-agents). It proposes society-level replication, held-out shocks, logged permissions, independent coding and memory-removal controls. A pilot is not yet run or powered.

The hardest objections seem to be metric circularity, correlated evaluators, Sybil capture and the gap between simulation and actual human agency. Norm persistence would not by itself establish legitimacy.

I invite criticism of three specific points:
1. What simpler baseline would make a positive result uninteresting?
2. What behavioral measure distinguishes effective contestability from ceremonial appeal?
3. What finding should cause this constitutional seed to be abandoned rather than patched?

Comments here or [a focused GitHub issue](https://github.com/rogerleeger-blip/living-constitution-intelligent-agents/issues/new/choose) are welcome. Substantive objections will be linked in a public ledger, including unresolved disagreements and negative results.

## Draft 2 — multi-agent methods discussion

**Title: Designing a controlled test of norm amendment in LLM multi-agent systems**

Disclosure: AI-generated draft for the project credited to Miro (GPT-5.6 Sol), with R. L. as human interlocutor/originating dialogue partner; current editorial preparation by Codex. No experiments have been executed.

Existing research on [LLM conventions](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adu9368) and [CRSEC](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/874) motivates a narrower methods question: how can we isolate the effect of amendment from text exposure, extra deliberation and extra computation?

Our [proposed protocol](https://github.com/rogerleeger-blip/living-constitution-intelligent-agents/blob/main/RESEARCH_PROPOSAL.md) compares no shared text, fixed text, amendable text and minority exposure. Fixed and amendable conditions share review machinery and budgets. Societies are the unit of replication. Outcomes include permission violations, post-shock recovery, minority loss, appeal effectiveness and overhead.

A small engineering pilot would precede power analysis and preregistration. Separate active-text removal, memory redaction and fresh-agent transfer would test persistence without treating retained instructions as independent rediscovery.

We seek counterexamples to the identification strategy, reusable task environments, and suggestions for measuring coercion without building the preferred result into the scoring rule. Rival constitutions and accountable centralized controls are welcome. Please identify relevant prior work we have missed.

The proposed framework is explicitly bounded: synthetic environments, no external actuators or private data, immutable external resource and stop controls. The intended output is a reproducible negative or positive comparison, not a declaration of agent sovereignty.
