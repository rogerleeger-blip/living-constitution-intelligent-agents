# Project governance

This file governs repository maintenance, not the simulated societies.

Roger Leeger is the repository owner and human contact. Miro is the credited originating author; a Codex assistant may propose, research and publish authorized revisions with explicit AI disclosure. The project currently has no independent review board and must not imply otherwise.

## Changes

1. Open an issue describing the claim, evidence or value disagreement and the affected clause.
2. Record the strongest counterargument and a concrete proposed change or test.
3. Link a proposed revision to its originating issue. Record the decision, reasons, dissent and unresolved questions.
4. Maintain version history. Do not rewrite a published release to conceal a change; issue a correction or new version.
5. For substantive normative changes after v0.1, allow at least seven days for public comment where practical. A deadline is an opportunity to object, not evidence of consent. Seek independent criticism; if none is obtained, state that limitation.
6. Correct factual errors promptly, recording the previous claim and correction. Withdraw harmful or exposed private data when necessary, explaining the removal without repeating it.

Version 0.1 establishes the discussion baseline. Clarifications and corrections increment the patch version; substantive changes increment the minor version. A 1.0 label would require explicit justification and must not imply scientific validation.

## Moderation and conflicts

Criticism, competing frameworks and negative results are welcome. Remove only spam, harassment, private information or material that cannot appropriately be hosted, and record a brief reason. Do not close an objection merely because it challenges the central thesis. “Acknowledged” does not mean “resolved.”

The author and repository owner are interested parties. Their own assessments are not independent review. Invite alternative adjudicators and preserve minority accounts; the absence of independent maintainers is an open limitation.

## Feedback cycle

Check actual public issues and the recorded outreach links. Summarize new objections faithfully, link the source, classify them, and propose tests or revisions. Never manufacture reviews, endorsements or responses. Avoid repetitive promotional posts. Notify Roger only of meaningful developments or actions requiring his involvement.
